How the Patch Pals got here

All five met at the Interplanetary Fix-It Fair — the place you go when your planet has a problem too big to fix alone. A passing comet told them about a garden on Earth where people keep their minds bright by playing together. So they shared a teapot-shaped ship called The Kettle, and landed softly in Bright Patch. Here is how each of them got to the Fair — and why each one stayed.

Boop heard his family's oldest song drifting off into space, fainter every night. He hopped aboard a humming shooting star and chased it all the way to the Fair.

He stayed because people here still sing songs back to each other. Every echoed melody goes into his brass jar — and home to Planet Chime, where the quiet is finally filling up with music again.

Boop's favorite study is the science of sound — how humming makes the air wiggle and how echoes travel. When the Kettle flies, he tunes its hum so the engine purrs on key.

Squish took the biggest bounce in her planet's history — off Trampoline Hill, straight through a shy cloud, and clean out of the sky. She landed at the Fair with a happy squeak.

She stayed because the garden is full of things to cheer for: raindrops, sprinklers, splashy games. Every cheer she mails home makes one shy cloud blush — and blushing clouds are how the rain comes back.

Squish studies weather — clouds, rain, and the water cycle. She picks the perfect flying day, and she can smell rain coming three gardens away.

Munch smelled the Fair's snack stand from three planets away and followed his nose the whole way there. He came for the snacks. He stayed for something better.

In the garden he learned what Crumb had forgotten: seeds become orchards. Now his napkin travels home each week wrapped around seeds instead of crumbs — and three new orchards are already growing.

Munch studies plants — how a seed becomes a sprout becomes an orchard. He grows the mint for the Kettle's tea, because the ship won't fly on cold tea and everybody knows it.

Zuzu arrived at the Fair on official business: delivering a crate of lost mittens that had drifted to Planet Tidy. Then she saw a poster that said HELP WANTED: FUN — and her three eyes went wide.

She stayed because the garden is tidy AND joyful at the same time — proof it can be done. Every week she unboxes one of Tidy's forgotten games and mails home the instructions, filed under OPEN OFTEN.

Zuzu loves math — counting, measuring, and sorting things into patterns. Every repair starts with her tape measure, and every shelf in the ship is labeled in her neat handwriting.

Comet fell asleep inside a parcel on speedy Planet Zoom and was express-delivered to the Fair before he woke up. It was the best accident of his life.

He stayed because the garden moves at noticing speed. Every slow morning walk becomes a postcard home marked 'read slowly' — teaching the fastest planet that together beats first.

Comet studies the stars — orbits, speed, and star maps. Astronomy taught him his favorite lesson: even the fastest things are best watched slowly. He charts every trip home.

The Kettle

The Kettle wasn't always a spaceship. It was an old soup kettle at the Fix-It Fair's lost-and-found, dented and forgotten, until five little strangers needed a ride to Earth. They polished it, bolted on three portholes, and it flew on the very first try — powered by one cup of warm tea.

The Kettle is an old ship, and old ships forget: it loses parts in the hedges and sometimes forgets the way home. The Pals never mind. They tie labels on everything, Boop hums it the way back, and Comet fetches whatever rolls away. A ship that forgets is still a ship that flies.

The Kettle is the crew's engineering classroom — gears, bolts, and how parts fit together. When something rattles, six heads lean in, and that's the point.

Six different planets, six favorite sciences — sound, weather, plants, math, stars, and engineering — and not one of them could fly the Kettle alone. Every trip takes all of them, which is exactly how the Pals like it. Different is what makes the team work.
